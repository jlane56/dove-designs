Client Process Guide

For: Justin Lane

The Dove Design Co.

This is your step-by-step playbook for taking a client from first inquiry to live website. Follow it in order. Use the checklists, email templates, and questions as your guide — adjust to fit each client's personality and scope.

8 Phases
Start → Launch
1

Phase One

Inquiry Comes In

Respond within 24 hours

Someone fills out your contact form, DMs you, or gets referred your way. This is your first impression — be warm, fast, and professional. Your goal here isn't to sell, it's to get them on a discovery call.

  • Read their message carefully and note the type of project (new build, redesign, maintenance) Check if they have an existing site, a logo, and written copy ready — this affects scope.
  • Reply within 24 hours — faster is better A quick response builds confidence before the call even happens.
  • Invite them to a free 30-minute Discovery Call with 2–3 available time options
  • Log their name, business, and contact info somewhere you can find it

2

Phase Two

Discovery Call

30 minutes · Free · No pitch

This is the most important conversation you'll have. Listen more than you talk. Your job is to understand their business, their goals, and their customers — not to impress them with technical knowledge. A good call makes writing the proposal effortless.

  • Show up 2 minutes early — have a notepad or doc open to take notes
  • Let them talk first — ask an open question like "Tell me about your business"
  • Listen for: who their customers are, what they struggle with, what success looks like
  • Gauge their comfort with tech — affects how much hand-holding they'll need
  • Ask about timeline — when do they want to be live?
  • Ask about budget range (gently) Try: "Do you have a rough budget in mind, or are you open to hearing what makes sense?" This helps you know which package fits.
  • Before ending: confirm you'll send a proposal within 48 hours

Questions to Ask

  • What does your business do, and who do you serve?
  • Do you have an existing website? What do you like or hate about it?
  • What's the #1 thing you want your website to do for you? (book clients, sell products, look legit?)
  • Are there any sites you love the look or feel of?
  • Do you have a logo? Brand colors? Any existing photography?
  • Do you have written copy (text) for the site, or will you need help with that?
  • What's your ideal launch timeline?
  • Have you worked with a web designer before? How did it go?

3

Phase Three

Proposal & Contract

Send within 48 hours of discovery call

Strike while the iron is hot. The proposal is your first real deliverable — it should be clean, specific, and easy to say yes to. Be clear about what's included, what isn't, and exactly what happens next.

  • Write a custom proposal — restate what you heard on the call, then outline scope This shows you listened. It builds trust before work has even started.
  • Include: pages, features, number of revision rounds, timeline, and price
  • Clearly list what's NOT included (copywriting, photography, logo design, domains) Scope creep kills profit margins. Protect yourself upfront.
  • Send a contract — even a simple one. Protects both of you.
  • Invoice for 50% deposit upfront — do not start work without it Remaining 50% due at launch (or split: 25% mid-project, 25% at launch).
  • Follow up once after 3 days if no reply

What to Include in the Proposal

  • Project overview — 2–3 sentences recapping what they told you
  • Deliverables — exact pages and features (homepage, contact, about, etc.)
  • Timeline — start date, review date, estimated launch
  • Investment — total price + payment split
  • What's not included — copywriting, stock photos, domain, hosting
  • Revision policy — 2 rounds of revisions included, additional at $X/hr
  • Next steps — sign contract + pay deposit to kick things off
4

Phase Four

Onboarding & Assets

After signed contract + deposit received

Before you can build anything, you need their raw materials. Send a clear list of everything you need — then wait for it before starting. Chasing assets mid-build derails timelines and your focus.

  • Logo file (SVG or high-res PNG preferred)
  • Brand colors (hex codes) if they have them
  • Photos (professional or phone — you'll work with what you have)
  • Written copy for each page If they don't have it, set a deadline or offer to write it for extra $.
  • 3–5 websites they like for reference/inspiration
  • Domain login (if they own one) or prompt them to buy one
  • Any existing hosting accounts
  • Social media handles to link to

Asset Collection Email — Send This

  • Keep it simple: a numbered list of exactly what you need
  • Give them a clear deadline: "I'll need everything by [date] to stay on our timeline"
  • Offer to hop on a quick call if they're confused about anything
!

Don't start building until you have at least 80% of the assets. Building a placeholder site and then retrofitting content wastes hours. The copy especially — it determines layout, section length, and navigation structure.

5

Phase Five

Design & Build

1–3 weeks depending on scope

This is your zone. Build it with care — mobile-first, fast, and clean. Keep the client informed with brief updates but don't over-communicate. One progress check-in when you're about halfway through is usually enough.

  • Set up the project folder and Git repo before writing a single line of code
  • Build desktop layout first, then test mobile throughout Check at 1280px, 900px, and 375px (iPhone) at minimum.
  • Use real copy and photos — never placeholder text in a client review
  • Check all interactive elements work: nav, forms, buttons, accordion, links
  • Run Lighthouse in Chrome DevTools — aim for 90+ on Performance and Accessibility Open DevTools → Lighthouse → Run. Fix whatever scores low.
  • Send a mid-build update to the client — one paragraph, "here's where things stand"
  • Deploy a preview link (Vercel is already connected — just push to GitHub)

Pre-Review Quality Check

  • All text matches the copy they gave you (no typos, no placeholder)
  • Every link goes somewhere — no dead hrefs
  • Contact form submits and you receive the email
  • Logo shows clearly on both light and dark backgrounds
  • Site looks good on iPhone (Safari) and Android (Chrome)
  • Page title and meta description are set correctly
  • No console errors in browser DevTools
6

Phase Six

Client Review & Revisions

5–7 days for client review window

Share the preview link and walk through the site together on a video call if possible. Seeing their reaction live tells you more than an email ever will. Collect all feedback in one batch — not dribbled in over days.

  • Send the preview link with a loom or short written walkthrough A 3-minute Loom video walking through the site dramatically reduces back-and-forth.
  • Ask them to consolidate all feedback into one email or doc — not in pieces over Slack/texts "Please send me all your feedback in one email by [date] so I can handle everything at once."
  • Remind them what's in scope: 2 rounds of revisions are included
  • Implement changes — then confirm with them in writing what was updated
  • Get written approval before launching "Just reply 'approved' and I'll get this live for you." — Keep it simple.

Managing Revision Requests

  • In scope: Text changes, color tweaks, layout adjustments, swapping photos
  • Out of scope (extra charge): Adding new pages, building new features, rewriting all copy, logo redesign
  • If a request is out of scope, say: "That's outside our original scope — I can do it for an additional $X, or we can save it for a future phase."
7

Phase Seven

Launch Day

After written approval + final payment received

Don't launch until the final invoice is paid. This protects you and gives the client a natural deadline to wrap up revisions. Launch day should feel like a celebration — send a great email, make them feel the moment.

  • Confirm final payment has cleared before going live
  • Point the domain to Vercel (or their host) Vercel: Project Settings → Domains → Add your domain. DNS propagation takes up to 48 hrs but usually under 30 min.
  • Verify SSL certificate is active (https:// shows green padlock)
  • Final QA on the live domain — not just the preview link
  • Check on mobile one more time after the domain is connected Sometimes things shift slightly on production vs preview — especially fonts and images.
  • Send the launch email (see template below)
  • Celebrate with them — it's a big deal for a small business

Pre-Launch Final Checks

  • Site loads on https:// with no certificate errors
  • Old site (if any) has been redirected or replaced
  • Contact form works and emails arrive at the right address
  • Google Search Console — submit sitemap if applicable
  • All pages have correct page titles and meta descriptions
  • Social share image (OG image) is set so links look good when shared

Phase Eight

Post-Launch & Ongoing

30 days after launch + ongoing

The work isn't really done at launch. A quick check-in 30 days out builds loyalty, surfaces any issues, and naturally opens the door to ongoing maintenance or future work. Happy clients refer people — this phase is how you turn a project into a relationship.

  • 30-day check-in: email asking how the site is performing and if they need anything Keep it short. "Just checking in — how's the site treating you?"
  • Ask for a testimonial or Google review Best time to ask: right after the launch excitement, while they're still thrilled. Make it easy — give them a direct link.
  • Offer a monthly maintenance plan if it feels like a fit
  • Stay in touch — like their posts, send a quick "love what you're doing" message a few months in Small businesses remember who actually showed up for them.
  • Add their site to your portfolio (with permission)

Ongoing Maintenance Options (offer these)

  • Basic care — ~$75/mo: Uptime monitoring, security updates, one small content change per month
  • Growth — ~$150/mo: Above + quarterly page updates, SEO tweaks, priority response
  • À la carte: $X/hr for one-off updates — best for clients who rarely need changes
✦

Referrals are your best lead source. Every happy client is a walking advertisement. A thoughtful check-in, a genuine relationship, and great work are the three things that fill your pipeline without paid ads.
